The feasibility of applying quantum computing to portfolio optimization is the central focus of this work, whose purpose is to evaluate whether current quantum hardware can offer real advantages over classical methods in realistic financial scenarios. Portfolio optimization is a central problem in quantitative finance. Classical methods such as the Markowitz approach provide widely accepted solutions, while more recent alternatives such as Hierarchical Risk Parity (HRP) introduce approaches based on clustering and correlation distances.

Quantum computing opens new possibilities for the representation and processing of financial information. In this context, this work proposes the implementation and experimental validation of the Quantum Hierarchical Risk Parity (QHRP) algorithm, which integrates quantum data encoding through a variational ansatz with hierarchical risk ordering techniques. The objective is to assess whether this formulation enables alternative representations of the correlation space and to compare its behaviour with the classical HRP method.

The formulation of the quantum circuit used as the proposed solution (ansatz), the financial data encoding scheme, the circuit architecture with 5 and 6 qubit configurations, and the hybrid processing workflow are presented. Experiments are carried out on both quantum simulators and real IBM quantum hardware, providing hardware validation on actual Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) devices subject to noise, limited qubit counts, and gate errors. The stability of the encoding, the reproducibility of the results, and the limitations of the approach are evaluated, experimentally demonstrating that the quantum hierarchical structure persists beyond ideal simulation. The results indicate that QHRP achieves better risk-adjusted performance in the conducted experiments, exhibiting structural coherence in the sectorisation of assets by risk factors, albeit with a high computational cost in simulation. This cost does not transfer to real quantum hardware, where a fixed gate-depth circuit executes in constant time regardless of the number of qubits.
